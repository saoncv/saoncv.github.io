Biography & Overview

I am a Senior Petroleum Engineer and Consultant at Petrobras with over 17 years of industrial experience in offshore oil and gas production systems, Artificial Lift (ESP/BCSS), Multiphase Flow, Boosting, and Flow Assurance. Concurrently, I serve as a researcher and candidate professor at the Department of Energy, School of Mechanical Engineering (FEM), State University of Campinas (UNICAMP).

My academic trajectory includes a Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering (2022) and M.Sc. in Petroleum Science and Engineering (2018) from UNICAMP, along with post-graduate specializations in Petroleum Engineering (Petrobras University) and Financial Economics (UNICAMP). My research bridges fundamental multiphase fluid dynamics, wave propagation, vibration-based sensing, and physically-informed artificial intelligence for offshore production monitoring.

Research Axes & Innovation

Multiphase Flow & Slug Dynamics

Experimental characterization and two-fluid modeling of intermittent slug flow in horizontal, inclined, and annular pipes. Two-state Markov chain modeling for hydrodynamic pressure fluctuation signatures.

Gas-Liquid Separation & Artificial Lift

Hydrodynamic modeling of drift-flux fields inside centrifugal separation systems (ESP Skids / BCSS). Optimization of subsea boosting, heavy oil elevation, and gas-liquid separation units.

Fluid-Structure Interaction (FSI) & Non-Invasive Sensing

Analytical wave propagation and non-invasive vibration sensing for multiphase flow regime identification, void fraction estimation, and flow-induced vibration (FIV) mitigation without intrusive sensors.

Physically-Informed AI & Digital Oilfield

Hybrid modeling combining multiphase fluid physics with data-driven machine learning algorithms for real-time production monitoring, digital twins of oil wells, and carbon capture & storage (CCS) systems.
